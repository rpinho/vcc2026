## Headline

1. **Our fidelity is below the coin-flip line.** In every one of our entries visible on the board, the raw `direction_fidelity_yield_raw` is below 0.5, the chance point:

   | Entry | Raw fidelity |
   |---|---:|
   | v8a | 0.4933 |
   | v9a | 0.4923 |
   | SLIM | 0.4981 |

   For comparison:
   - **All** of the top-60 teams sit between 0.517 and 0.564.
   - The local H1 harness agrees: B0's raw fidelity is 0.4487.

   The official brief states that "Random directions score 0.5 in expectation once coverage reaches 1".

   Our layout calls about 885 genes per target. The val-A median n_real is 46 (cell-eval2 `docs/metrics.md` L1825). So n_pred ≫ n_real, and fidelity is simply **the sign precision of our call set**, and it is worse than random.

   Across the whole board, scaled fidelity ≈ 3.34 × (raw − 0.513) (fit over 1,421 entries). Lifting raw fidelity from 0.492 to the top-team level of about 0.53 is therefore worth about +0.13 scaled, or **about +0.02 overall**. That is the largest DE-side gain on the table, and it needs no new biology.

2. **Calls are compared against the measured control (`CONTROL_SOURCE_SCORED = "real"`).** The DE side reads only two things:
   - Wilcoxon ranks of per-cell CPM values (target sum 1e6).
   - The sign of the **arithmetic mean of per-cell CPM**. This is not the pseudobulk ratio that MSE and PDS read.

   A gene the reference does not call still counts toward k through the sign of its reference fold change.

## Ranked ideas

### 1. Audit the signs of our call set: find out why precision is below 0.5, then fix it

- **Sources:**
  - cell-eval2 brief §3–4, https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ArcInstitute/cell-eval2/main/docs/vcc2026_metrics/vcc2026-metrics-brief.md (fetched 18:46:57Z): "F_p = k / max(n_pred, n_real) … Random directions score 0.5".
  - Our raw values: board snapshots 10-19, 10-20 and 10-26.
  - Top-60 values: `raw/ideas_1026/top60_from_snapshot_130923Z.txt`.
- **Evidence it raises the score.** All public strong entries have positive sign precision; we are at −0.008 against chance. Moving to +0.03 is worth about +0.02 overall (the slope above).

  The pattern in our own data:
  - The official comparison (v5b dense against v9a at 885 calls) moved fidelity **up** by +0.037 scaled.
  - The local comparison (D1770 against B0) also moved it up.
  - Under F = precision, extra calls raise F only when the **marginal** calls are more sign-accurate than the call set's average. When the average is below 0.5, almost any near-random call does that.

  So the density "gain" on fidelity is mainly a symptom of a below-chance head.
- **Targets:** fidelity first. Reach shares the same signs, ranked over reference-significant genes only.
- **How it plugs in.** On the local harness, for each called (target, gene), record:
  - (a) the sign the model intended (the sign of the predicted log-fold response);
  - (b) the scorer's sign, from the arithmetic mean of per-cell CPM of the rendered cells against the measured control;
  - (c) the reference sign.

  Then split by: reference-significant or not; up or down; control-CPM bin; and call rank decile.

  If (b) ≠ (a) on a measurable share of genes, the renderer is flipping signs. One way this can happen: pinned group sums or depth-correlated placement move the CPM arithmetic mean against the pseudobulk. The fix is a per-gene sign constraint in the layout.

  If (a) ≠ (c) mostly on genes the reference does not call, apply idea 2.
- **Effort:** half a day for the diagnostic, which is label-free on the rendered side and uses H1 truth for (c). The fix inside the layout is 0.5–1 day.
- **Risk:** low for the diagnostic. The H1 harness exaggerates fidelity magnitudes (R7 correction), but the sign of a below-chance result is what matters here.

### 2. Skew-aware call direction: on low-confidence genes, prefer down calls and drop weak up calls

- **Source:** our own simulation, `raw/ideas_1026/null_sign_skew_sim.txt`. The script is my own, not downloaded. It uses the scorer's lfc definition (brief §3: arithmetic mean of per-cell CPM, ε = 1e-9).
- **What the simulation shows.** For a gene with **no true effect**, a perturbed group of n cells has reference lfc < 0 with this probability:

  | Group size | Expression (UMI/cell) | P(lfc < 0) |
  |---|---|---|
  | 50 | 0.05–0.1, overdispersed | 0.53–0.56 |
  | 100 | low | 0.52–0.54 |
  | 400 | low | 0.51–0.52 |

  The cause is right skew in counts and in library size. The sample mean of a skewed distribution sits below the population mean more often than above. In addition, all-zero groups give lfc = −∞, which still carries a direction.

  This has not been checked against real cells. It is a mechanism that can push up-heavy weak calls below 0.5 and down-heavy weak calls above it.
- **Targets:** fidelity (k on genes the reference does not call). Jaccard is untouched.
- **How it plugs in:**
  1. Resample n_p cells from each D/E/F control set, with n_p taken from `pert_counts`, many times. For each gene, measure P(mean CPM of the resampled group < mean CPM of the rest). This is the context's null-sign prior. It is fully label-free.
  2. In the fixed-budget layout, give a call to (target, gene) only when P(match) = P(true sign correct) × P(the effect dominates) + P(null) × null-prior(sign) is above the current average precision.
  3. Weak up calls on low-expression genes will usually fail that test; replace them with down calls or with higher-confidence genes.

  Keep n_pred at or above the predicted n_real (see idea 5).
- **Effort:** half a day.
- **Risk:** medium. The size of the effect depends on real dispersion. Check it on H1 (with idea 1) before relying on it.

### 3. Depth-aware call reassignment at a fixed budget (Skadi's "mixs75")

- **Source:** leaderboard description by Skadi's Seaborn Farm, entry `20261019-mixs75` (first seen `lb-2026-10-20T044920Z.json`): "k098 + call-side order 0.25 rank d5 + 0.75 rank depth-aware expected z (S1), |AUC| multiset and signs kept, calls matched to k098". See `raw/ideas_1026/teams/historical_descriptions_top50.txt`.
- **Evidence.** This is a single-factor step against the team's previous entry (10-18 23:04 → 10-19 22:51). The call count was matched.

  | Component (scaled) | Before | After |
  |---|---:|---:|
  | Fidelity | .038 | .049 |
  | Reach | .258 | .271 |
  | Jaccard | .008 | .016 |
  | PDS | .792 | .793 |
  | MSE | .388 | .390 |
  | NMAE | .236 | .236 |
  | **Overall** | **.2867** | **.2924 (+0.0057)** |
- **Targets:** fidelity, reach and jaccard together, with MSE and PDS untouched.
- **How it plugs in.** Keep our budget and the multiset of effect sizes and signs. Change only **which genes** receive the calls. Rank genes by the expected **reference** Wilcoxon z, computed from:
  - the predicted lfc;
  - the gene's control CPM, which sets its detectability;
  - n_real cells.

  Their blend was 0.75 on this and 0.25 on predicted-delta rank. Genes the reference is likely to call significant are the ones with a defined sign that is not noise. They add to jaccard, and they head the reach ordering.
- **Effort:** 0.5–1 day.
- **Risk:** low to medium. The evidence is one entry on another team's pipeline.

### 4. Reach: rank sign-certain genes first, with no change in call count

- **Sources:**
  - cell-eval2 `docs/metrics.md` L1786–1825 (fetched 18:46:57Z): "vcc2026 no longer charges a submission for predicting each gene's habitual direction … the habitual-direction head estimates at 0.0748, +0.038 of the member's span … +0.006 on avg_score for no target-specific skill".
  - Brief §5: "only a wrong call at the very first position can drive k* to zero".
  - FlowState's board history (`teams/score_histories.txt`).
- **Evidence:**
  - Arc's own estimate is +0.006 overall from the head alone, with no target-specific skill.
  - FlowState went from 10-18 16:03 to 10-19 19:37 with reach .246 → .308 while PDS, MSE, NMAE, fidelity and jaccard stayed flat. Overall rose .3194 → .3301 (+0.011).
  - Our reach is .186 scaled; ranks 10–50 sit at about .27.
- **Targets:** reach. It is computed only over reference-significant genes, ordered by the submission's (p_adj, p, |lfc|).
- **How it plugs in.** Reach depends only on **order**, not on the number of calls. In the layout, give the smallest p-values (first 10–20 positions) to the genes that are most likely to be reference-significant **and** sign-certain:
  - strong source responders with cross-source sign agreement;
  - downstream genes with near-deterministic direction;
  - genes in the context's habitual direction.

  The largest predicted |lfc| is not the right criterion. Below the call threshold, keep p-values graded in confidence order, so the reach prefix keeps extending past the call set.
- **Effort:** a few hours on top of the v9 ordering.
- **Risk:** low. The PDS and MSE channels are untouched.

### 5. Per-target call budget from predicted response size, instead of a uniform 885

- **Sources:**
  - Nadig et al., TRADE, Nat Genet 2025, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11244993/ (lit receipts #1): "a typical gene perturbation affects an estimated 45 genes, whereas a typical essential gene perturbation affects over 500"; response size is "correlated across cell types (average correlation = 0.62"; growth-essential genes are enriched "by 4.22x".
  - cell-eval2 `vcc2026-metrics.md` L419: "Calling more genes than the reference found buys nothing, since the coverage term saturates; calling fewer costs proportionally."
- **Evidence:**
  - D1770's local gain was concentrated in a few large, under-called targets: the top 5 targets gave 38% of the summed gain (DECISION R6/R7).
  - Jaccard is about |∩|/n_pred when n_pred ≫ n_real. With a val median n_real of 46, uniform 885 calls caps jaccard on small targets. X-team leads jaccard (.092 scaled).
- **Targets:**
  - Fidelity: coverage on targets where n_real exceeds the budget.
  - Jaccard: fewer surplus calls on small targets.
- **How it plugs in.** Set budget_p ≈ an upper quantile of predicted n_real. Predict it from the target's source response size (TRADE-style impact, not source DE counts), its essentiality and its knockdown strength. Then:
  - over-call where n_real could exceed 885;
  - trim small targets to roughly 1.5–2× predicted n_real.

  Do the trimming only after idea 1. While our call-set precision is below chance, trimming keeps the head, and the head is the problem.
- **Effort:** half a day.
- **Risk:** medium. The only official density comparison (v5b against v9a) favoured dense on fidelity and jaccard and lost on reach. Sequence it after ideas 1–4.

### 6. MSE: check whether the across-perturbation budget B binds, and if it does, raise the spread in the pseudobulk channel only

- **Sources:**
  - Brief §2 (fetched 18:46:57Z): N_p = (1/G_p)(‖b̂−b‖² − ρ·min(Ĉ_p, C_p) − C_p), with ρ = min(1, B / Σ min(Ĉ, C)), where B is the centred across-perturbation sum of squares of **our** pseudobulks.
  - `delta.py` #348 block, residual 2: "(1 − 2α)‖d‖² keeps improving past α = 1 … until the budget crosses the claim"; "B_real/claim_max = 2.047 / 1.715 / 2.050 on val A / B / C".
  - T3V200 docs: counts under "an energy budget with kappa 0.97" (DAILY-10-26).
  - Skadi `skadi-sings` (`20261021-skadi-cs-pgain-c06-key-loo`).
- **Evidence:**
  - Skadi's step from 10-20 23:41 to 10-21 21:07 raised scaled MSE .388 → .434, with PDS .804 → .805, NMAE .233 → .236, fidelity flat at .051 and reach .264 → .272. Overall rose +0.0099. What "pgain" means is not disclosed.
  - The jackknife is "46–55%" of the raw measured distance on the val panels (`docs/metrics.md` L486–490), so credit lost to under-dispersion or a small B is large.
  - Our raw normalized MSE is 0.91; the top-60 sit at 0.46–0.61.
- **What it means for our pipeline.** With pinned, noise-free group sums, the numerator reduces to Σ Δ² − min(B, Σ min(Ĉ_p, C_p)):
  - If **B binds**: the within-group layout is irrelevant to MSE, and the only route is more truth-aligned spread in b̂.
  - If B does not bind: the layout decides, through Ĉ.
- **Targets:** MSE, and PDS through the same pseudobulk.
- **How it plugs in:**
  1. Compute B and Σ min(Ĉ_p, Ĉ_ctrl at n_p) on the **prediction side** for B0. This needs no labels: approximate C_p by the jackknife of n_p resampled control cells. The scorer also logs ρ and warns when budget/claim < 0.7.
  2. If ρ < 1, amplify only the pseudobulk channel, the group sums that MSE and PDS read, up to B ≈ 0.95–0.97 × claim. Keep the per-cell CPM arithmetic means, which DE lfc and NMAE read, at their calibrated amplitude. Decouple the two through depth-correlated count placement (the dual-moment idea).

  v8b collapsed NMAE because it scaled both channels together. This plan does not.
- **Effort:** half a day for the ρ readout. The decoupled emitter is 1–2 days, and v7a's code is still on disk.
- **Risk:** medium-high. This is close to the closed amplitude line; what is new is the B/claim readout and the single-channel restriction.

### 7. Keep Ĉ_p at or above C_p on every row: cell count and gene-selective dispersion

- **Source:** cell-eval2 `docs/metrics.md` L645–668: "Hold every per-(p,g) sum fixed and redistribute counts across the group's cells: the realized ‖μ̂−μ‖² cannot change, while the delete-1 jackknife Ĉ CAN … an under-dispersed submission forfeits the correction and reads worse … The under-dispersion penalty is intended (Alex, 2026-08-15)." Also `delta.py`: "Predicted cells per perturbation are NOT constrained".
- **Evidence:** our v8a was a layout-only change and lost 80% of scaled MSE (0.082 → 0.016, OFFICIAL_SCORES). That is exactly this mechanism.
- **Targets:** MSE, while DE power is kept.
- **How it plugs in.** C sums over about 18k genes, while our calls cover at most about 900 per target. Keep realistic, control-like dispersion on all genes that are not called, so Ĉ ≥ C_p. Keep tight cells only on the called genes.

  Do not emit more cells per target than the reference has (n from `pert_counts`). Ĉ scales roughly as 1/n, so extra cells shrink the correction.

  Gate every layout by Σ min(Ĉ, Ĉ_ctrl at n) ≥ B. This applies in the non-binding case from idea 6.
- **Effort:** half a day for the gate. The selective layout is 1 day; v7b-style code exists.
- **Risk:** medium. Selective scatter can cost Wilcoxon power on borderline calls.

## Denser calls and within-group layout: what the public mechanics say

- **Fidelity.** F = k/max(n_pred, n_real). Our 885 calls are far above the val median n_real of 46, so F is our sign precision, currently below 0.5. Denser calls help F only in two ways:
  - (a) coverage on targets with n_real above the budget;
  - (b) when the marginal calls are more accurate than the average.

  (b) is true for us only because the average is below chance. The official v5b>v9a and local D1770>B0 fidelity gains fit this reading. **Fix the head's signs (ideas 1–2) before buying density.** After that, density beyond about n_real is neutral at constant precision and costs jaccard.
- **Reach** reads only the ordering of reference-significant genes. The call count matters only through the p-values the layout produces. FlowState and Arc's head estimate show that reach can be bought by ordering alone (idea 4).
- **Jaccard** falls roughly as 1/n_pred once n_pred ≫ n_real.
- **MSE.** The layout matters only through Ĉ_p, and only while Σ min(Ĉ, C) is below the budget B. Above that point, extra scatter is worthless. Below it, every unit of Ĉ lost is MSE lost; the under-dispersion penalty is by design. If our B binds (ρ < 1), the layout cannot help MSE at all, and only pseudobulk spread can (idea 6).

  D1770's raw MSE ratio of 0.994 is consistent with a layout that sits near the non-binding edge. Compute ρ and the claim for B0 and D1770 directly; both are prediction-side quantities.
