VERDICT: **B — recipient-control-state conditioning.** Replace the unavailable recipient response basis explicitly; do not pretend it was estimated.
EXPERIMENT: Fit a ridge-regularized, low-rank bilinear map using recipient control-PC scores × donor pooled-response-PC scores, predicting coefficients in a training-only pooled response basis.
PROTOCOL: Run eight outer family-held-out folds. Fit response PCs, control-PC loadings, preprocessing, and map parameters on seven families; project the held-out family’s legal controls into that control embedding. Select rank/regularization by inner family-held-out validation. Score direction_fidelity and signature Jaccard across eight folds; score full-incumbent official composite only for CD4/H1.
KILL TEST: Reject recipient conditioning if it fails to improve held-out direction_fidelity and signature Jaccard over a matched state-ablated map. Reject deployment if mean full-incumbent composite gain across CD4/H1 is nonpositive; report both folds separately.

**Justification:** B preserves recipient specificity without requiring forbidden treated responses. Its response axes come from training families; recipient controls condition the transformation rather than identify recipient response PCs. That makes the formulation estimable, although biological usefulness remains an empirical question. Strong regularization matters because eight families provide little independent evidence for state-dependent mapping, regardless of row count. The state-ablated comparison tests whether controls actually help rotation rather than merely adding capacity. Two incumbent-scored folds support only a limited deployment gate—not an eight-family composite claim.
