# DECISION 2026-10-24 21:50 ET — second-recipient layout replication, not a density sweep

## What changed in my reading tonight
The earlier "adaptive per-target budget is dead" note rested on two things I rechecked at the source:

1. **The fidelity numerator is direction agreement, not intersection.** `de_direction_fidelity_yield_raw`
   is `k / max(n_pred, n_conf)` where `k` counts *sign agreement* on adjudicable predicted-significant
   genes. I first recomputed it with truth-significance intersections and got 4.67 instead of 0.4533;
   the verified `layout_audit.json` reproduces **0.4533327423559518** exactly under the correct rule.
2. **Under-calling is charged against a fixed denominator.** For the 26/79 targets with
   `n_conf > n_pred`, the denominator is `n_conf`, so adding calls is close to free there. That
   contradicts the earlier "shrinking cannot help, therefore budget is closed" framing.

## Why I did NOT launch a density sweep
An H1-only model of intermediate budgets (1200/1600/2100/2700/3500) is **monotone in every component** and
would recommend going straight to the dense endpoint. But the dense endpoint is already an official
measured loss: v5b native ~4,475 calls scored **0.1528** against v9a 885 calls at **0.16135**. So the
H1 frontier model is contradicted by the only official observation at the endpoint. A sweep whose
predicted optimum sits exactly where the official data says it is worse would be fitting H1, not
measuring a transferable effect. **It would also be a second single-recipient layout decision**, which is
the defect this whole thread keeps hitting.

## What I launched instead
`_codex/vcc_layout_cd4_1025.md` -> `vcc2026/layout_cd4_1025/`: the **paired v9-vs-native layout
