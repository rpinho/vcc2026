**VERDICT:** Run A — but as a distributional experiment. Your error is in spread, not location; B (embeddings, graph prior) and C (gating) only move the mean, and B's unseen-group payoff isn't your stated failure. A, reframed as per-domain noise-scale estimation, is cheap, directly attacks the stated gap, and its outcome tells you whether domain heteroscedasticity is the bottleneck at all.

**EXPERIMENT:** Hypothesis: residual variance differs materially across the 8 domains, so per-domain inverse-variance weights plus a per-domain scale on the predictive distribution will cut proper-scoring loss by more than the mean-error gain. Compare baseline (per-domain mean + residual NN, fixed Gaussian/quantile head) vs. the same model with (i) inverse-variance domain weights and (ii) per-domain σ (or quantile offsets) fitted on inner held-out residuals.

**PROTOCOL:** 5-fold CV stratified by domain, nested: inner fold estimates residuals/weights, outer fold scores. Primary metric: CRPS (or mean pinball loss over q∈{0.1,0.25,0.5,0.75,0.9}); guardrails: MAE and per-domain calibration (PIT coverage at 80%). Repeat 3 seeds; report fold-wise paired differences. Fits in well under two CPU-days; use remaining time to log per-domain variance ratios.

**KILL TEST:** Abandon if the max/min per-domain residual-variance ratio is <2 (nothing to exploit), or if CRPS improves <3% relative with the paired 95% CI crossing zero, or if MAE degrades >1%. If killed, the signal points to within-domain input-dependent noise → next test is a heteroscedastic/quantile head, not B or C.
